Current reform tracker

What is changing in Sierra Leone’s health system?

A dated research record of major national reform signals. Official plans are separated from verified implementation, and older claims are superseded when better evidence appears.

Current as of: September 2026Evidence status: Public with qualifiers

Strategic plan

Validated

NHSSP 2026–2030

WHO’s Q2 2026 reporting says the National Health Sector Strategic Plan 2026–2030 was aligned and validated, creating a unified five-year roadmap. This is a stronger status than the late-2025 development-stage reporting.

WHO Q2 2026 ↗

Benefit package

Mapping complete

Basic UHC Health Benefit Package

Nationwide mapping was reported complete in Q2 2026, providing an evidence base for development of an equitable, costed Basic Universal Health Benefit Package. The final package and financing arrangements should not be assumed before publication and implementation are verified.

WHO Q2 2026 ↗

Health financing

In development

National Health Financing Strategy 2026–2030

WHO reported in May 2026 that a new financing strategy was being developed as Sierra Leone seeks stronger domestic financing and reduced donor dependence.

WHO · May 2026 ↗

UHC institution

Status to verify

Proposed Sierra Leone Agency for UHC

A 2025 draft bill proposed integrating the Free Healthcare Initiative and SLeSHI under a dedicated UHC agency. This site will not call the agency enacted or operational until later legal and implementation evidence confirms that status.

WHO · July 2025 ↗

Maternal & child health

Implementation signal

Triple Zero facility supervision

WHO’s Q2 2026 newsletter reports district taskforces active in all 16 districts and supportive supervision reaching more than 100 health facilities, surfacing commodity stockouts, cold-chain failures and staffing gaps, with many corrected during the process.

WHO Q2 2026 ↗

Community health

Existing policy

National CHW Policy 2021

The policy specifies community mapping, household registers, routine household visits, referral and stronger CHW–PHU linkage. Sierra Leone Health Research treats these as existing national policy features whose implementation and reliability should be studied.

How to read this tracker

National direction is evidence context, not endorsement.

The tracker helps keep research current and prevents outdated policy assumptions from being repeated. A validated strategy can still face implementation gaps; an announced reform can still remain unimplemented.
