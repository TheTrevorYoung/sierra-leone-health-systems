Sierra Leone implementation diagnosis

Sierra Leone already has community-based health architecture. The question is why it does not work reliably enough.

Community health workers, PHUs, district structures, referral pathways and national primary-care policies already exist. Research must distinguish the architecture on paper from the conditions that make it work in practice.

The diagnosis

The research problem is implementation, not absence.

A community-health policy can be sound and still produce inconsistent results if supervision, staffing, supplies, financing, transport, information or accountability fail.

We therefore treat every apparent “gap” as a research question. The goal is to identify which conditions are actually constraining performance, how they interact, and which are most important to fix before adapting ideas from elsewhere.

Diagnosis dimensions

What has to be understood before transferability can be judged.

Workforce + supervision

Availability, skills, distribution, workload, compensation, management and retention.

PHU capability

Staff, medicines, diagnostics, power, water, information, equipment and escalation capacity.

Supplies + logistics

Procurement, stock continuity, storage, distribution and last-mile reliability.

Financing

Household cost, provider incentives, recurrent affordability and national financing compatibility.

Referral + transport

Decision, departure, transport, receiving capability, return of information and follow-up.

Governance + data

Who sees failure, who can correct it, how performance is measured and how accountability works.

Comparison comes after diagnosis

What works elsewhere matters only if we know what Sierra Leone would need.

The transferability test starts with the Sierra Leone baseline. A mechanism from Cuba, Rwanda, Costa Rica or another system is useful only if its enabling conditions can be identified and the local gaps can be realistically addressed.
