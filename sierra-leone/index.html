Sierra Leone baseline

Understand the system that exists before redesigning it.

The project starts with Sierra Leone’s current CHW, PHU, district and referral architecture—what policy intends, what current evidence shows, and what remains unknown.

Policy / official plan

What design says

National strategy and reform documents describe a stronger primary-care system, community delivery, workforce professionalisation, improved financing, digital systems and progress toward universal health coverage.

Implementation evidence

What evidence must test

Policy adoption does not prove medicines are in stock, referrals close, staff are present, information returns or households can afford care. Those are implementation questions.

Unknown

What we still need to learn

Catchment-level reliability, real referral completion, staff workload, supply continuity, patient costs, transport failure and the full economic cost of fixing weak links.

System map

From community to referral care

CommunityHouseholds, community health workers, prevention, early identification
PHU / first-contact careEssential primary services, continuity, medicines, diagnostics, routine escalation
Higher-capacity primary-care supportAdvanced diagnostics, supervision, selected services, stabilization
District / referral hospitalHigher-acuity care, surgery, obstetric emergencies, specialist services
Return & follow-upResults, discharge information, medication changes and continuity back to the responsible team

Current reform context

Design for national compatibility.

The emerging 2026–2030 planning cycle emphasizes primary health care, community service delivery, workforce, infrastructure, supply chains, financing, digital health and UHC. The project therefore aims to strengthen and test the national architecture—not create a parallel boutique system.
