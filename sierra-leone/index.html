Sierra Leone baseline

Understand the system that exists before redesigning it.

We start with Sierra Leone's current community, PHU, district and referral architecture—what policy intends, what evidence shows, and what still needs to be measured.

Policy / official plan

What design says

National strategy and reform documents describe stronger primary care, community delivery, workforce development, financing, infrastructure, supply chains and digital systems.

Implementation evidence

What evidence must test

Policy adoption does not prove medicines are in stock, staff are present, referrals complete, information returns or patients can afford the journey.

Unknown

What we still need to learn

Catchment-level reliability, referral completion, workload, supply continuity, patient costs, transport failure and the real cost of fixing weak links.

System map

From community to referral care

CommunityHouseholds, community health workers, prevention, early identification
PHU / first-contact careEssential primary services, continuity, medicines, diagnostics, routine escalation
Higher-capacity supportAdvanced diagnostics, supervision, selected services and stabilization
District / referral hospitalHigher-acuity care, surgery, obstetric emergencies and specialist services
Return & follow-upResults, discharge information, medication changes and continuity back to the responsible team

Research lens

Reliability is visible in the journey.

A health system can look complete on an organizational chart and still fail the person moving through it.

The research therefore focuses on connections: whether first-contact care is dependable, whether escalation occurs early enough, whether transport and receiving capacity exist, and whether information and follow-up return after referral.

Current reform context

Research for national compatibility.

The 2026–2030 planning cycle emphasizes primary health care, community service delivery, workforce, infrastructure, supply chains, financing, digital health and UHC. Our research asks how reliability can be strengthened within that national architecture—not through a parallel boutique system.
