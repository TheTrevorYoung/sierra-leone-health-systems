Tracer pathway

Maternal Health Stress Test

Maternal care is our first end-to-end tracer because the system has to connect early identification, routine care, risk recognition, transport, emergency capability, information return and postnatal follow-up.

Sierra Leone, 2010 · Robert Yates/DFID · CC BY 2.0 · historical reference image

The research question

Can the whole pathway work for one person, end to end?

A maternal-health guideline can be clinically sound and still fail in practice. The tracer asks whether every operational link needed to turn that guideline into care actually holds.

StageWhat should happenFailure question
1. Pregnancy identifiedPregnancy enters a continuity pathway early enough for routine care and risk assessment.Who notices if no one registers the pregnancy?
2. Antenatal continuityScheduled monitoring, prevention, screening and counselling occur reliably.What happens after a missed visit?
3. Risk recognitionFront-line staff identify higher-risk cases and escalate them before an emergency.Is risk detection linked to an actual receiving pathway?
4. Delivery / routine referralThe delivery setting matches clinical need, and the receiving site is known.Can the patient reach the right level at the right time?
5. Emergency escalationDanger signs trigger rapid clinical action, transport and a facility able to receive the patient.What if transport, blood, theatre, staff or acceptance fails?
6. Postnatal follow-upMother and newborn return to a responsible team with information and a follow-up plan.Does discharge disappear into an information gap?

Why the tracer matters

It exposes system failure, not just clinical failure.

A correct referral decision is not the same thing as a completed referral.

Transport may not arrive. A receiving facility may lack blood, theatre capacity or staff. Information may never return. The person may leave hospital with no continuity back to the frontline team.

The tracer makes those dependencies visible and turns them into research questions.
