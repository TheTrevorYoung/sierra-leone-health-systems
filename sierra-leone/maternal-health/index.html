Maternal Health Stress Test

Maternal care is our first end-to-end tracer because one pathway can reveal whether early identification, routine care, risk recognition, referral, transport, emergency capability and postnatal follow-up actually connect.

StageWhat should happenResearch question
1. Pregnancy identifiedPregnancy enters a continuity pathway early enough for routine care and risk assessment.Who notices if no one registers the pregnancy?
2. Antenatal continuityScheduled monitoring, prevention, screening and counselling occur reliably.What happens after a missed visit?
3. Risk recognitionFront-line staff identify higher-risk cases and escalate them before an emergency.Is risk detection linked to an actual receiving pathway?
4. Delivery / routine referralThe delivery setting matches clinical need, and the receiving site is known.Can the patient reach the right level at the right time?
5. Emergency escalationDanger signs trigger rapid clinical action, transport and a facility able to receive the patient.Where can transport, blood, theatre, staffing or acceptance fail?
6. Postnatal follow-upMother and newborn return to a responsible care pathway with information and a follow-up plan.Does discharge disappear into an information gap?

Why this tracer matters

It exposes system failure, not just clinical failure.

A technically correct guideline is not enough if medicines are absent, referrals do not complete, transport cannot arrive, receiving capability is unavailable, information is lost or follow-up disappears. The tracer helps research where the chain breaks and what evidence is still needed.

Research boundary: Sierra Leone Health Research does not provide maternal care or medical advice. This page is a systems-research framework, not a patient-care pathway.
