About

Independent research into how health systems work.

Sierra Leone Health Research studies health-system reliability, comparative models and the gap between policy and operating reality, with an initial focus on Sierra Leone.

What we are

A pure research entity.

Our work is research, analysis, evidence synthesis, public explanation and expert review. We study where care systems succeed, where they break down, and what evidence from Sierra Leone and other countries can responsibly tell us.

What we are not

We do not provide medical care, medical advice or health insurance. We do not operate clinics, hospitals, referral networks or patient services, and we do not represent the Government of Sierra Leone.

Why this work became personal

Trevor Young

In 2014, as Ebola spread through Sierra Leone, I helped co-found Sierra Leone Action, a group of Sierra Leonean physicians, professionals and volunteers. We worked on a proposed convalescent serum/plasma response and the equipment and training needed to support it. I returned to Sierra Leone that November and spent roughly four months helping train healthcare workers and technicians on blood-separation devices.

That experience made health-system reliability a practical question for me. A medical idea is not enough by itself. Equipment has to arrive. People have to be trained. Approvals, logistics, coordination and trust have to line up.

Later experiences navigating care in Sierra Leone kept bringing me back to the same question: what happens when one part of the care journey works but another does not?

I am not a clinician. My role in Sierra Leone Health Research is founder, publisher, researcher, reporter, interviewer and convener. Clinical and technical claims belong with evidence and qualified review.
