About the institute

Independent research for a Sierra Leone-specific primary healthcare model.

We study why community-oriented primary healthcare works in some settings, diagnose the system Sierra Leone already has, and test what can realistically be adapted — without pretending the answer is known in advance.

Mission

Research what works — and what Sierra Leone would need.

Produce rigorous, understandable and implementation-useful research on effective community-oriented primary healthcare systems; diagnose the conditions that limit reliability in Sierra Leone; and develop an evidence-based Sierra Leone-specific model that can be tested, costed and challenged.

Vision

Reliable community-oriented primary healthcare rooted in Sierra Leone.

A Sierra Leone where every community can depend on a locally grounded primary healthcare system that is preventive, accountable, connected to higher levels of care, financially realistic and built on evidence rather than assumption.

Primary research question

What can transfer — and under what conditions?

What features of effective community-oriented primary healthcare systems are transferable to Sierra Leone, and what institutional, workforce, financing, infrastructure and operational conditions are required for those features to work reliably at scale?

Research approach

Cuba sparked the question. Comparative evidence has to answer it.

The inquiry began with a practical curiosity about how a resource-constrained country could organize primary care around communities, prevention and continuing responsibility.

Cuba is therefore an important starting comparator, not an ideology or a blueprint. Rwanda and Costa Rica broaden the comparison. Sierra Leone’s existing community-health and primary-care architecture is then used to test what is transferable, what conditions are missing, and what adaptation would be required.

Institutional purpose

Research that can move beyond description.

Our work is designed to identify practical, evidence-based approaches that could be tested, adapted, financed and implemented in Sierra Leone.

That does not mean Sierra Leone Health Research is a healthcare provider. The institute’s public role is research: comparison, diagnosis, transferability analysis, model development, costing, evidence translation and independent evaluation.

Research / implementation firewall

Any future patient-facing implementation would require a separate lawful operating structure and appropriate clinical governance, regulation, financing and safeguards. Research conclusions cannot be predetermined to justify implementation.

Founder

Trevor Young

In 2014, as Ebola spread through Sierra Leone, I helped co-found Sierra Leone Action, a group of Sierra Leonean physicians, professionals and volunteers. I returned to Sierra Leone that November and spent roughly four months helping train healthcare workers and technicians on blood-separation devices connected to a proposed plasma response.

That experience made health-system questions practical for me: an idea can be medically plausible and still fail if equipment, training, approvals, logistics, coordination and trust do not line up.

The present research question developed later from another curiosity: why do some resource-constrained countries organize community-oriented primary care more effectively than others, and what can Sierra Leone learn without simply copying them?

I am not a clinician. My role is founder, researcher, reporter, interviewer and convener. Clinical and technical conclusions belong with evidence and qualified professionals working within their actual expertise.
