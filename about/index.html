About

An independent health-systems research and public-learning initiative.

We study how healthcare delivery systems work, translate evidence for the public, and develop a Sierra Leone-specific model through comparative research, public input and expert review.

What we are

Research, translation and accountable model design.

Sierra Leone Health Systems is designed to make health-system research understandable enough for public participation while keeping technical decisions evidence- and expertise-governed.

What we are not

We do not currently provide medical care, medical advice, health insurance or government services. We are not an approved clinical pilot or licensed provider.

Why this work became personal

Trevor Young

In 2014, as Ebola spread through Sierra Leone, I helped co-found Sierra Leone Action, a group of Sierra Leonean physicians, professionals and volunteers. We worked on a proposed convalescent serum/plasma response and the equipment and training needed to support it. I returned to Sierra Leone that November and spent roughly four months helping train healthcare workers and technicians on blood-separation devices.

That experience made health-system reliability a practical question for me, not an abstract one. A medical idea is not enough by itself. Equipment has to arrive. People have to be trained. Approvals, logistics, coordination and trust have to line up.

Years later, helping people navigate care in Sierra Leone—and more recently supporting a vulnerable pregnant woman through a difficult period—kept bringing me back to the same problem: a person can still be failed when one part of the system works. Referral, transport, continuity, communication, follow-up and social support all matter.

I am not a clinician. My role in Sierra Leone Health Systems is to research, report, convene, test assumptions and make the model accountable in public. Clinical and technical questions belong with qualified professionals working within their actual expertise.
