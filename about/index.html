About

Independent research on how community primary care can work reliably in Sierra Leone.

We compare systems, examine Sierra Leone's existing health architecture, test transferability and publish what the evidence supports.

Mission

Produce research that is rigorous, understandable and useful.

Identify mechanisms that appear to work, diagnose Sierra Leone's implementation constraints and test what could realistically fit.

Vision

Better decisions built on evidence rather than assumption.

Research should clarify what Sierra Leone can adapt, what it should avoid and what conditions would have to change.

Where the inquiry began

Cuba raised the question. Comparison tests the answer.

The original curiosity was simple: how could a resource constrained country organize primary care around communities and prevention?

Cuba is not a blueprint. Rwanda, Costa Rica, Portugal and Thailand provide different tests of the same question.

Institutional boundary

Research is the work.

Sierra Leone Health Research does not provide medical care or operate healthcare services. The institution studies systems, publishes evidence and develops testable research conclusions.

If patient facing implementation is ever pursued, it should sit in a separate lawful operating structure so the research function can remain independent.

Founder

Trevor Young

My interest in Sierra Leone's health system became practical during the 2014 Ebola crisis, when I helped cofound Sierra Leone Action and returned to Sierra Leone during the response.

The experience reinforced a basic lesson: a promising idea can fail when training, equipment, logistics, approvals and coordination do not work together.

I am not a clinician. My role is founder, researcher, reporter, interviewer and convener. Clinical conclusions belong with evidence and qualified professionals.
