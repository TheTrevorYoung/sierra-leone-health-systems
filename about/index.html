About the institute

Research what works, then test what fits Sierra Leone.

We compare primary healthcare systems, study Sierra Leone's existing system and build a model from evidence.

Mission

Understand what makes community healthcare work.

Identify useful mechanisms, diagnose Sierra Leone's implementation gaps and test what can realistically transfer.

Vision

Reliable primary healthcare rooted in Sierra Leone.

A system that is preventive, accountable, connected and financially realistic.

Primary research question

What can transfer, and what has to be true for it to work?

Which community primary care mechanisms can work in Sierra Leone, and what workforce, financing, infrastructure, institutional and operating conditions do they require?

Where it began

Cuba sparked the inquiry.

The question grew from a simple curiosity about how a resource constrained country could organize care around communities and prevention.

Cuba is one comparison, not a blueprint. Rwanda, Costa Rica, Portugal and Thailand help test the idea from different settings.

Institutional boundary

Research first.

We compare systems, diagnose gaps, test transferability, estimate requirements and publish findings.

Research and implementation remain separate

Sierra Leone Health Research does not provide medical care. Any future patient facing implementation would require a separate lawful operating structure and independent safeguards.

Founder

Trevor Young

My interest in Sierra Leone's health system became practical during the 2014 Ebola crisis, when I helped cofound Sierra Leone Action and returned to Sierra Leone during the response.

The experience reinforced a simple lesson. A good idea can fail when training, equipment, logistics, approvals and coordination do not work together.

I am not a clinician. My role is founder, researcher, reporter, interviewer and convener. Clinical conclusions belong with evidence and qualified professionals.
