Methodology

Make uncertainty visible — and make every transfer claim earn its place.

The method separates country description from evidence, policy from implementation, and promising mechanisms from claims that they will work in Sierra Leone.

Comparative systems

Every country is examined through a common lens: population responsibility, community interface, primary-care capability, workforce, continuity and referral, financing, information, governance, evidence strength, weaknesses and tradeoffs. Country reputation is never evidence by itself.

Sierra Leone implementation diagnosis

The research first maps what Sierra Leone already has. Policy and official design are separated from implementation evidence and unknowns. The objective is to identify which institutional and operating conditions explain the difference between intended architecture and reliable performance.

Transferability

Promising mechanisms move through a sequence: define the mechanism, establish the evidence, identify enabling conditions, map the Sierra Leone equivalent, identify gaps and adaptation requirements, assess workforce/cost/governance feasibility, and assign a confidence status. A mechanism can be rejected at any stage.

Evidence and claim classes

Documented fact, policy or official plan, implementation evidence, expert interpretation, lived experience and project hypothesis are kept distinct. Policy is not proof of implementation. Expert opinion is not empirical evidence. AI may assist research operations but is not evidence.

Participation and expert review

Reviewers are matched to defined questions, credentials are verified where relevant, conflicts are disclosed and disagreement is retained. Lived experience can identify problems and research questions, but it does not determine clinical standards by vote. Human-participant research requires the appropriate ethics and governance process.

Versioning and correction

Material changes are logged with the previous position, new evidence or input, the decision and the reason. Research conclusions must remain capable of changing — including when evidence contradicts the founder’s preferred hypothesis.
