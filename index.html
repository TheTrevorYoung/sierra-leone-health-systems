Why do some countries make community healthcare work — and what can Sierra Leone learn from them?

We compare community-oriented primary healthcare in Cuba, Rwanda, Costa Rica and elsewhere, study the system Sierra Leone already has, and test which mechanisms could realistically work here — and under what conditions.

Where the question began

Cuba sparked the inquiry. It does not provide the answer.

The starting curiosity was simple: how can a resource-constrained country organize primary care around communities, prevention and continuing responsibility?

That question led beyond Cuba. Rwanda and Costa Rica use different structures, political systems and financing arrangements, yet each offers mechanisms worth examining. The research asks what actually produces reliable performance — and what depends on conditions Sierra Leone does or does not have.

Comparative systems

Three starting cases. One disciplined question.

We do not rank countries or copy their organizational charts. We isolate mechanisms, identify enabling conditions, examine weaknesses and ask whether the idea can survive Sierra Leone's reality.

The Sierra Leone question

Sierra Leone is not starting from zero.

Community health workers, PHUs, district structures, referral pathways and national primary-care policies already exist. That changes the research problem.

We need to understand what works reliably, what exists mainly in policy, where implementation varies, which enabling conditions are weak or missing, and why existing community-oriented structures do not consistently deliver the intended result.

Primary research question

What can transfer — and what has to be true for it to work?

What features of effective community-oriented primary healthcare systems are transferable to Sierra Leone, and what institutional, workforce, financing, infrastructure and operational conditions are required for those features to work reliably at scale?

Governance + accountability

Transferability, not copying

A foreign model is not a recommendation.

A mechanism enters the Sierra Leone model only after we understand the evidence behind it, the conditions it depends on, the local equivalent, the gaps, the adaptation required and the cost and governance implications.

Research connected to reality

Evidence should lead to practical questions.

Our research is designed to move beyond description. We aim to identify practical, evidence-based approaches that could be tested, adapted, financed and implemented in Sierra Leone.

Sierra Leone Health Research remains the research institution. Any future patient-facing implementation would require separate lawful operating structures and independent safeguards.

Build the evidence with us

Serious research should attract serious people.

We are building a network of researchers, clinicians, nurses, health workers, health economists, implementation specialists and institutions able to challenge assumptions, add evidence and improve the Sierra Leone model.
