The problem we own

Reliability across the whole care journey.

The central question is not whether Sierra Leone has policies, facilities or committed health workers. It is whether a person can move through prevention, first-contact care, referral, treatment and follow-up without the chain breaking.

A

Accountability

Does a defined team know which households it is responsible for?

P

Primary care reliability

Are essential services, medicines, diagnostics and staff consistently available?

R

Referral completion

Can patients reach higher-level care and return with information and follow-up?

F

Financial protection

Can the model work without shifting unsustainable costs onto patients?

Build the Model With Us

Evidence, expertise and lived experience each have a different role.

Community members can describe what care feels like. Frontline workers can expose operational reality. Researchers can challenge assumptions. Qualified experts can review technical questions. None of these routes substitutes for professional standards or regulatory authority.

What changed

Decision 01

Referral success redefined

Success is not “a referral was issued.” It is documented completion, information return and follow-up.

Decision 02

Financing separated from fundraising

Cash budget, full economic cost, recurrent sustainability and replication affordability are different tests.

Decision 03

NO IMPLIED PERMISSION

Interest, meetings or informal support never substitute for the authorization required to deliver regulated care.

Pilot pathway

Research first. Clinical launch only after gates close.

A future pilot is an ambition subject to evidence, professional governance, regulation, institutional agreement and full financing. It is not currently an operating health service.
