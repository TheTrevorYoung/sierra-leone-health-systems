Transferability Framework 0.x

Something that works elsewhere is not automatically a solution for Sierra Leone.

We test mechanisms against the conditions that make them work. Only then can a finding become part of a Sierra Leone-specific primary healthcare model.

Research boundary: This is a research and design framework, not a clinical implementation plan or authorization.

The transfer test

Seven questions between observation and recommendation.

The framework is deliberately demanding. It is designed to stop attractive foreign examples from becoming premature “best practices” before their evidence, dependencies, costs and Sierra Leone fit are understood.

01

Define the mechanism

Describe the specific practice or system function rather than the country label.

Question: What exactly appears to produce the benefit?

Definition
02

Establish the evidence

Separate policy description, observational evidence, evaluation, expert interpretation and hypothesis.

Question: How confident are we that the mechanism contributes to the outcome?

Evidence
03

Identify enabling conditions

Map workforce, financing, infrastructure, supplies, management, information, governance and social conditions.

Question: What has to be true for this mechanism to function?

Conditions
04

Map Sierra Leone

Find the existing policy, workforce, facility or operational equivalent and measure the difference.

Question: What already exists and how reliably does it work?

Local baseline
05

Identify adaptation

Determine what would need to change rather than assuming transplantation.

Question: Which gaps are realistic to close and which may make transfer unsuitable?

Adaptation
06

Test feasibility

Examine workforce, cost, financing, governance, regulation, logistics, risk and implementation capacity.

Question: Can the adapted mechanism be sustained and governed safely?

Feasibility
07

Enter the Sierra Leone model

Only supported mechanisms become versioned model hypotheses, with uncertainty and change history visible.

Question: What evidence would confirm, narrow or overturn the hypothesis?

Model 0.x

Example mechanisms under study

The country is not the unit we copy. The mechanism is the unit we test.

Population responsibility

Defined communities

Team design

One mechanism, not the thesis

Closed-loop referral now sits where it belongs.

Referral continuity remains important, but it is one component of a much larger community-primary-care question. The framework studies it alongside workforce, PHU capability, financing, supplies, information and governance.

Model discipline

The model must be able to lose.

A mechanism can be rejected because evidence is weak, the enabling conditions cannot be built, the adaptation is unsafe, costs are unrealistic, or a better alternative exists. Research independence requires that outcome to remain possible.
