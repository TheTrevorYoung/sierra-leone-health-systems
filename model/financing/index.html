Model economics

Financing the model

This is an explanatory research page, not a fundraising page. The project will not call a pilot sustainable merely because grants can pay the first bill.

01

Cash budget

What money must actually leave the pilot’s accounts.

02

Full economic cost

The value of all resources used—including government staff, buildings, donated commodities, vehicles or subsidized inputs.

03

Recurrent sustainability

Whether routine operating costs can continue without unstable emergency fundraising.

04

Replication affordability

Whether another catchment could reproduce the model at realistic cost.

Donor-dependence firewall

A pilot can be funded and still be economically false.

One-time grants may be appropriate for design, equipment, evaluation or transition. They should not hide recurrent staffing, supply or service costs that no plausible institution can sustain.

No speculative price claims

No subscription fee, insurance premium, cost per patient or guaranteed reimbursement will be published before the model is actually costed and the financing mechanism is verified.

Sierra Leone financing baseline

The financing problem is already measurable.

The latest National Health Accounts figures summarized by WHO for 2023 show that households and donors each financed a large share of total health expenditure. These figures describe the national context; they are not a budget for this project.

Current reform status: WHO reported in May 2026 that a National Health Financing Strategy 2026–2030 was being developed. In Q2 2026, WHO also reported completion of nationwide Health Benefit Package mapping. The project should therefore avoid locking a parallel financing mechanism before these national reforms are clearer.

Economic decision rule

Four separate questions

  1. Solvency: Can the pilot pay its bills for the full planned period?
  2. Sustainability: Can recurrent costs continue after the initial funding arrangement?
  3. Affordability: Can the model be replicated at the intended scale?
  4. Compatibility: Can financing fit Sierra Leone’s national UHC and PHC direction rather than create a parallel system?
