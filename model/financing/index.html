Health financing research

What does reliable care cost—and who carries that cost?

Financing is studied as a health-system mechanism: how household payments, public financing, donor dependence and recurrent costs affect access, continuity and long-term reliability.

01

Household burden

What patients and families pay directly, including costs outside the clinic such as transport and time.

02

Full economic cost

The value of all resources used, including staff, buildings, commodities, vehicles and subsidized inputs.

03

Recurrent sustainability

Whether essential services can continue without unstable or exceptional financing.

04

Scale affordability

Whether a mechanism remains plausible when considered beyond a single facility or catchment.

Research discipline

A funded intervention is not automatically a sustainable one.

One-time grants can obscure recurrent staffing, supply or maintenance costs. Research should expose those costs rather than treating donor financing as proof of economic viability.

Research questions

Four economic tests

  1. Access: How do direct and indirect costs change whether people complete care?
  2. Sustainability: Which recurrent costs create the greatest reliability risk?
  3. Affordability: Which mechanisms remain plausible at meaningful scale?
  4. Compatibility: How do findings fit Sierra Leone's national UHC and PHC financing direction?
