Research collaboration

Help us build a better answer — and challenge it.

We are building a serious research network around one question: which community-oriented primary healthcare mechanisms could work in Sierra Leone, and what conditions would make them reliable?

Who we need

Expertise that improves the work, not decorative titles.

Clinicians, nurses, midwives, CHWs, PHU and hospital operators, researchers, health economists, data and supply-chain specialists, implementation leaders and institutions can contribute evidence, critique, methods and operational knowledge.

Comparative research

Study a system or mechanism

Help assess evidence from Cuba, Rwanda, Costa Rica or another relevant setting using the common comparison framework.

Sierra Leone diagnosis

Explain operating reality

Add evidence or professional context on CHWs, PHUs, workforce, medicines, diagnostics, financing, referral, data or governance.

Transferability

Stress-test an assumption

Challenge whether a mechanism can survive Sierra Leone's workforce, financing, infrastructure, institutional and operational conditions.

Economics

Test cost + sustainability

Help separate attractive design from realistic recurrent cost, affordability and financing.

Research boundary

Collaboration is not healthcare access.

  • Do not submit emergency medical requests.
  • Do not request diagnosis or treatment.
  • Do not post identifiable patient information.
  • Do not use public GitHub issues for confidential or sensitive material.
  • Structured human-participant research requires the appropriate protocol and governance.
Koidu Hospital, 2006 · L. Lartigue/USAID · Public domain · historical reference image

Why build the network now?

Research quality and implementation knowledge reinforce each other.

The immediate purpose is better research: stronger sources, better questions, sharper critique and more accurate Sierra Leone context.

Over time, the same disciplined network also builds the expertise and practical knowledge required to understand what responsible real-world testing would demand — without converting the research institute into a healthcare operator.
