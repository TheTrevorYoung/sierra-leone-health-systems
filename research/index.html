Research

Study the system. Compare the evidence. Publish what we find.

Our research focuses on how Sierra Leone’s health system functions in practice, where continuity and reliability fail, and which lessons from other systems may be relevant.

Baseline

Sierra Leone

Understand the system that already exists: policy, institutions, PHUs, CHWs, hospitals, workforce, financing and operating evidence.

 Research framework

Referral reliability

Study the full chain from first contact and referral decision through arrival, receiving care, information return and follow-up.

 Systems tracer

Maternal Health Stress Test

Use pregnancy-to-postnatal care to examine whether multiple parts of the system work together.

 Comparative method

Healthcare That Works

Compare mechanisms across countries without treating any national model as a blueprint.

 Research Note 01

Minimum Reliable PHU

Examine what “reliable” primary-care capability should mean using Sierra Leone facility and workforce evidence.

 Audit trail

Evidence Library

Sources, dates, evidence classes, limitations, corrections and where each source is used.

Comparative rule

We study mechanisms. We do not copy countries.

Political systems, workforce supply, financing, geography and institutions differ. A useful comparison asks what appears to work, why it works, the conditions it depends on and whether those conditions exist in Sierra Leone.

Current research shift

More Sierra Leone implementation evidence.

Published policy and international comparisons are only part of the picture. The next research priorities are real referral pathways, PHU reliability, frontline practice, workforce conditions, facility operations, medicines, diagnostics, transport and costs.
