Flagship comparative research

Healthcare That Works

Why do some countries make community-oriented primary healthcare work — and what can Sierra Leone learn from them without pretending a foreign system can simply be copied?

Formal research question

What can transfer, and under what conditions?

What features of effective community-oriented primary healthcare systems are transferable to Sierra Leone, and what institutional, workforce, financing, infrastructure and operational conditions are required for those features to work reliably at scale?

Research doctrine

Mechanisms, not country admiration.

A country enters the research because it helps answer a defined question. Every case is examined for strengths, weaknesses, evidence quality, enabling conditions, costs, tradeoffs and limits of transfer.

Starting comparator

Cuba

The original curiosity: defined populations, prevention, family doctor–nurse teams, continuity and polyclinic support.

African comparison

Rwanda

Community health workers, health posts, health centres, referral and the supporting system around them.

Integrated primary care

Costa Rica

Multiprofessional community teams, geographic responsibility, prevention, management and integrated networks.

Common comparison lens

Ask the same questions of every system.

Population responsibilityWho is accountable for whom, and how is that responsibility defined?
Community interfaceHow do prevention, outreach and first contact connect to formal care?
Primary-care capabilityWhat staff, medicines, diagnostics and infrastructure must work?
Workforce designWhat skills, supervision, incentives and workload assumptions support the model?
Continuity + referralHow does responsibility persist when patients move between levels?
FinancingWho pays, what incentives are created and how is recurrent cost sustained?
Information + measurementHow does data follow patients and inform management?
GovernanceWho can see failure, correct it and hold the system accountable?

The rule that matters

Similar structures do not guarantee similar results.

A community health worker programme, primary-care facility or referral policy cannot be evaluated by its name alone. The research must identify the institutional and operating conditions that turn architecture into reliable performance.

Transfer test

MechanismConditionsSierra Leone fitDecision rule

1. Isolate the mechanism

Describe the specific function that appears to matter. “Cuba,” “Rwanda” or “Costa Rica” is not itself a mechanism.

2. Identify enabling conditions

Map the workforce, financing, infrastructure, supplies, management, information, governance and social conditions that support the mechanism.

3. Test Sierra Leone fit

Find what Sierra Leone already has, where implementation differs, which gaps can realistically be closed, and what adaptation would be required.

4. Make the mechanism earn its place

A feature enters the Sierra Leone model only when the evidence and feasibility justify it. Weak evidence, unrealistic cost, unsuitable conditions or a better alternative are valid reasons to reject it.

Research outcome

The goal is not a Cuban, Rwandan or Costa Rican model.

The goal is a Sierra Leone-specific primary healthcare model built from comparative evidence and Sierra Leone’s own implementation reality.
