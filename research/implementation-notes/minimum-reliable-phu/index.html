Implementation evidence note · 01

A PHU is not reliable because a building exists.

The useful question is whether the people, medicines, equipment, basic infrastructure and everyday operating conditions needed for care are actually available when care is needed.

Model link: Minimum Reliable PHULast reviewed: 6 September 2026

Question

What should “reliable” mean at primary-care level?

Counting facilities tells us where buildings exist. It does not, by itself, tell us whether a person can receive dependable care there.

Plain English

A health unit should count as reliable only if the essentials needed to do its job are present and working—not merely because it appears on a facility list.

Historical implementation baseline

The 2018 Service Delivery Indicators measured capability, not just presence.

The Sierra Leone survey sampled 536 health facilities, used rosters covering 5,055 workers for absenteeism measurement, and assessed 829 health workers using patient-case simulations.

Its health indicators included provider effort and knowledge as well as basic inputs such as medicines, equipment, water, toilets and electricity.

Workforce reliability

A staff list is not the same as an accountable workforce.

A 2023 scoping review of Sierra Leone’s unsalaried health workers found evidence connecting unsalaried staffing arrangements with problems involving equitable access, informal charging, accountability and workforce planning.

The review synthesised 36 texts. It is evidence about a structural workforce problem, not a current 2026 staffing census.

Model implication

Minimum Reliable PHU should be a capability test.

PeopleAre required staff present, appropriately skilled, supervised and operating within a workable employment structure?
Medicines + suppliesAre essential items in stock when patients need them?
Basic infrastructureDo water, sanitation and electricity meet the minimum requirements for the services being offered?
EquipmentIs essential equipment present and usable, rather than merely recorded as procured?
Clinical capabilityCan the team recognise, manage and escalate the conditions within its intended service package?
ContinuityCan referral, information return and follow-up connect the PHU to the rest of the care journey?

What this evidence supports

The principle is strong; the current national measurement is not yet complete.

The evidence supports measuring PHU reliability as a bundle of operational capabilities rather than treating facility existence as success.

What this note does not prove

  • It does not estimate current 2026 nationwide PHU reliability.
  • It does not claim every facility has the same constraints.
  • It does not infer causation from the unsalaried-workforce review.
  • It does not use patient-level or identifiable health information.

Next evidence step

Move from an old national snapshot to current implementation evidence.

The next version should compare current, verified Sierra Leone facility and district evidence against this capability test: staffing, medicines, equipment, infrastructure, referral and follow-up. Where evidence is unavailable, the gap should remain visible rather than being filled by assumption.
