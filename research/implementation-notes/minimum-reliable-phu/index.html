Sierra Leone implementation study · 01

A PHU is not reliable because a building exists.

The research asks which workforce, medicines, diagnostics, equipment, infrastructure, supervision and escalation conditions must work consistently for first-contact care to be dependable.

Why this matters to transferability

Community-oriented primary care depends on a capable first-contact layer.

Cuba, Rwanda and Costa Rica organize their front-line systems differently, but none of those mechanisms can be meaningfully adapted if the local point of care lacks the capability required to perform its intended function.

Plain English

A health unit should count as reliable only if the essentials needed to do its job are present and working — not merely because it appears on a facility list.

Historical implementation baseline

The 2018 Service Delivery Indicators measured capability, not just presence.

The Sierra Leone survey sampled 536 health facilities, used rosters covering 5,055 workers for absenteeism measurement, and assessed 829 health workers using patient-case simulations.

Its health indicators included provider effort and knowledge as well as basic inputs such as medicines, equipment, water, toilets and electricity.

Workforce reliability

A staff list is not the same as an accountable workforce.

A 2023 scoping review of Sierra Leone’s unsalaried health workers found evidence connecting unsalaried staffing arrangements with problems involving equitable access, informal charging, accountability and workforce planning.

The review synthesised 36 texts. It is evidence about a structural workforce problem, not a current staffing census.

Capability test

Minimum Reliable PHU should be a bundle of operating conditions.

PeopleAre required staff present, appropriately skilled, supervised and operating within a workable employment structure?
Medicines + suppliesAre essential items in stock when patients need them?
Basic infrastructureDo water, sanitation and electricity meet minimum requirements for intended services?
Equipment + diagnosticsAre essential tools present, functioning and supported by consumables?
Clinical capabilityCan the team recognise, manage and escalate conditions within its intended service package?
ManagementAre supervision, data, accountability and problem correction functioning?
ContinuityCan referral, information return and follow-up connect the PHU to the rest of the system?

What this supports

Reliability is an operating capability, not an asset count.

The evidence supports further study of PHU reliability as a bundle of capabilities rather than treating facility existence as success.

What it does not prove

  • It does not estimate current nationwide PHU reliability.
  • It does not claim every facility has the same constraints.
  • It does not infer causation from the unsalaried-workforce review.
  • It does not establish the final service package or staffing model.

Next evidence step

Build a current Sierra Leone capability baseline.

Future work should compare current verified facility and district evidence against the capability test: staffing, supervision, medicines, diagnostics, equipment, infrastructure, data, referral and follow-up. Missing evidence should remain visible rather than being replaced by assumption.
