Country model study

Rwanda

Rwanda is a high-value African comparison because community health workers, health posts, health centres and referral hospitals are deliberately connected across levels of care.

Brief status: Under reviewLast reviewed: 6 September 2026

Why study it

Mechanisms before mythology.

This page is not an endorsement of an entire national system. It isolates design features that may be useful for Sierra Leone and records the conditions those features require.

Claim boundary

“Appears transferable” means worth deeper testing. It does not mean “should be copied.”

What appears strong

  • Community health workers are integrated into a broader delivery architecture rather than treated as a stand-alone volunteer layer.
  • Health posts bring basic services closer to communities and interface with health centres.
  • Referral levels are explicit, making access and escalation part of system design.
  • The current national health strategy continues to set explicit workforce, facility-readiness and health-outcome targets.

Limits & cautions

  • Rwanda’s governance, financing and administrative execution capacity are not identical to Sierra Leone’s.
  • Published access improvements do not prove every quality or continuity problem is solved.
  • Sierra Leone must test workforce payment, supervision and supply reliability rather than assume they transfer automatically.
  • Private-sector participation in health posts raises incentive and regulation questions that require separate analysis.

Transferability test

What would have to be true in Sierra Leone?

CHW integrationCan Sierra Leone strengthen CHW supervision, scope and accountability inside PHU-linked teams?
Intermediate access pointWould an upgraded PHU or hub reduce access gaps without duplicating existing structures?
Referral architectureCan routine and emergency escalation be made explicit and measurable?
District managementWhich management functions need district ownership for replication?
