Country model study

Cuba

Cuba is a foundational comparison because its primary-care architecture assigns responsibility to local teams, links those teams to polyclinic support, and emphasizes prevention and continuity.

Brief status: Under reviewLast reviewed: 6 September 2026

Why study it

Mechanisms before mythology.

This page is not an endorsement of an entire national system. It isolates design features that may be useful for Sierra Leone and records the conditions those features require.

Claim boundary

“Appears transferable” means worth deeper testing. It does not mean “should be copied.”

What appears strong

  • Defined territorial responsibility for local doctor–nurse teams.
  • Community-based first-contact care linked to a higher-capacity polyclinic.
  • Prevention and health situation analysis integrated with routine care.
  • Referral and community follow-up are designed as one continuum.

Limits & cautions

  • State structure, financing and workforce production differ materially from Sierra Leone.
  • PAHO/WHO currently documents medication and supply shortages, shortages of laboratory inputs and growing migration of healthcare personnel that strain service delivery.
  • A physician-heavy front line may be financially or operationally unrealistic for Sierra Leone at scale.
  • The useful lesson may be accountability and team design—not replicating institutional form.

Transferability test

What would have to be true in Sierra Leone?

Territorial responsibilityCould a named team own a defined household panel without creating an unaffordable staffing model?
Team compositionCan Sierra Leone use a mixed-skill team rather than copy a doctor–nurse ratio?
Hub supportCan existing or upgraded PHUs/hubs supply diagnostics, supervision and referral support reliably?
ContinuityCan information and follow-up return to the responsible team after referral?
