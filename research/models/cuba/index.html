Starting comparator

Cuba sparked the question. It does not provide the answer.

The original curiosity behind this research was how a resource-constrained country could organize primary care around defined communities, prevention and continuing responsibility. The task now is to separate useful mechanisms from the political, fiscal and workforce conditions that cannot simply be copied.

Why study Cuba

Mechanisms before mythology.

Cuba is useful because its primary-care architecture has historically connected local doctor–nurse teams to defined populations and polyclinic support while emphasizing prevention and continuity.

Research boundary

This is neither endorsement nor condemnation of the entire Cuban system. A mechanism earns attention only through evidence, and transferability must be tested separately.

Mechanisms worth testing

  • Defined territorial responsibility for local teams.
  • Community-based first contact linked to higher-capacity support.
  • Prevention and health-situation analysis integrated with routine care.
  • Referral and community follow-up treated as a continuum.

Enabling conditions + cautions

  • State structure, financing and workforce production differ materially from Sierra Leone.
  • Current PAHO/WHO reporting documents medication and supply scarcity, laboratory-input shortages and health-worker migration pressures.
  • A physician-heavy front line may be financially or operationally unrealistic for Sierra Leone at scale.
  • The transferable unit may be responsibility, team design or support architecture — not the institutional form itself.

Sierra Leone transfer test

What would have to be true here?

Defined responsibilityCould a named team own a defined community or household panel using Sierra Leone's existing community-health architecture?
Team compositionWhat mixed-skill team could deliver the function without copying Cuba's doctor–nurse ratio?
Primary-care supportWhich existing PHU, CHC or district functions could provide diagnostics, supervision and escalation support?
WorkforceWhat staffing, supervision, compensation and training conditions would be required?
FinancingWhat recurrent financing would make the mechanism sustainable without assuming Cuba's state structure?
ContinuityHow would information and responsibility persist after referral?

Research decision rule

Cuba can inspire a question and still fail the transfer test.

If a mechanism depends on workforce, financing, institutional or operating conditions that Sierra Leone cannot realistically build, it should be adapted or rejected. The research does not owe the original inspiration a favorable conclusion.
