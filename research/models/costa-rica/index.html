Country model study

Costa Rica

Costa Rica is useful because integrated multiprofessional primary-care teams combine public health, prevention and clinical care inside defined communities with support from higher-level teams.

Model status: Under reviewLast reviewed: September 2026

Why study it

Mechanisms before mythology.

This page is not an endorsement of an entire national system. It isolates design features that may be useful for Sierra Leone and records the conditions those features require.

Claim boundary

“Appears transferable” means worth deeper testing. It does not mean “should be copied.”

What appears strong

  • Multiprofessional community teams combine preventive, public-health and curative functions.
  • Teams are embedded in defined communities rather than operating as disconnected facilities.
  • Support teams extend capacity beyond the basic front line.
  • Primary care is linked to an organized referral network.

Limits & cautions

  • Costa Rica’s fiscal capacity, institutional history and social insurance structure differ from Sierra Leone’s.
  • Formal team composition may be too expensive or workforce-intensive to transfer directly.
  • Success depends on management, data and supply systems around the team—not the team name itself.
  • The financing architecture requires separate study before any payment mechanism is inferred.

Transferability test

What would have to be true in Sierra Leone?

Defined team panelCan a Sierra Leone team have explicit population responsibility?
Multiprofessional mixWhat minimum mix creates reliability without overstaffing?
Support layerCould a district or hub team provide specialist support without hospitalizing primary care?
Integrated preventionCan prevention and case finding become routine responsibilities rather than campaign-only activities?
